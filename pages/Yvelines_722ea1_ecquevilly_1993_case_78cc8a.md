## The night-time sighting and witness account

The original report begins in a domestic, rural-edge setting rather than with an already dramatic skywatching story. According to GEIPAN’s 2018 re-examination, the witness went to the gendarmerie on 24 November 1993, nine days after the sighting, and the gendarmes then carried out local enquiries, including checks around the witness and the environment. GEIPAN states that its file contained the gendarmerie preliminary report, two location plans, five photographs taken on site and four sketches made by the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The witness said he had been asleep when his two dogs woke him by barking in an unusual way. Looking out, he saw the dogs, and then his horses, facing the same direction. From another room, he saw lights at what he estimated as around five metres above the ground, lighting the ground below. His first thought was mundane: perhaps a helicopter was searching an area, because stolen vehicles were often found nearby. The oddity, in his account, was the silence. He opened the window and still heard nothing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The sighting then changed from static lights into a moving object-like impression. The lights reportedly moved towards the house, and when they reached overhead the witness described a luminous circular form with large lights of different brightness. The reported colours were white, yellow and slightly bluish, with dark-light-style flashes and bluish effects on the ground. The entire observation lasted about 20 to 25 seconds, including roughly ten seconds when the lights seemed stationary and the remainder as they moved towards him. When the witness went outside, the lights had vanished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

This combination is why the Ecquevilly report is more interesting than many brief night-light cases. It is not merely “a light in the sky”. The account includes animals reacting, an initial low apparent position, ground illumination, movement towards the observer, silence despite an open window, colour variation, a circular arrangement and a short but continuous sequence. Yet each of those details comes from one person’s perception during a brief event just after waking, which is exactly why the case remains evidence-limited as well as unusual.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Ecquevilly illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the file is stronger than a casual sighting

The strongest point in the file is not that the witness described something spectacular; it is that investigators had enough material to test parts of the account. GEIPAN’s case page gives the Ecquevilly file an unusual combination for a single-witness case: residual strangeness of 0.70 and consistency of 0.75. In GEIPAN’s method, “strangeness” means what remains hard to explain after comparison with known phenomena, while “consistency” depends on the quantity and reliability of the information available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-11-01335" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanECQUEVILLY (78) 15.11.1993 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The gendarmerie material mattered because it preserved the scene soon after the report. GEIPAN’s re-analysis used the witness sketches, gendarmerie photographs and site references to reconstruct the apparent direction and size more carefully than a bare narrative would allow. It also noted that the nearest local map in the original gendarmerie file was wrongly oriented, an example of the kind of practical archival problem that can affect old UFO cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Weather and aviation context were also checked. GEIPAN used Pontoise weather data for wind and Trappes data for cloud and visibility, noting a very lightly clouded sky, visibility falling from 10,000 metres to 8,000 metres and then 3,020 metres, and a weak north-westerly wind. The report also placed the witness in a busy aviation region west-north-west of Paris, noting nearby civil and military aerodromes, including Les Mureaux about 4 km away, Beynes-Thivernal about 13 km away, and Chavenay-Villepreux about 14 km away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

That context does not solve the case, but it sets the right standard. In Yvelines, a night-time object report has to be tested against aviation, weather, light effects and astronomical explanations before it can be treated as genuinely unresolved. Ecquevilly remains notable because GEIPAN did that basic checking and still did not find a satisfactory identification.

## What GEIPAN re-examined decades later

GEIPAN’s 2018 re-examination did not simply preserve the original D classification out of habit. The report says the case belonged to a wider set of older files being reviewed with newer tools and later investigative experience. That re-analysis narrowed the apparent scale of the phenomenon: GEIPAN concluded that the object or light formation was probably of modest apparent size, about five degrees at its closest, much smaller than a reader might assume from the witness sketches alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The helicopter hypothesis was the main conventional possibility because the witness himself initially thought of one. GEIPAN considered it weakly plausible in broad terms, then found several problems. The reported circular luminous form, number of lights, bluish colour, ground-light effect and lack of ordinary navigation or anti-collision lights all counted against a straightforward helicopter confusion. The silence was especially awkward: GEIPAN calculated that an EC145-type helicopter subtending about five degrees would have been roughly 55 metres away, close enough that it should have been heard in a quiet night-time setting with the window open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The report also rejected broader categories as poor fits. It found the described physical characteristics clearly incompatible with astronomical or meteorological explanations, and said that the number of lights and ground illumination argued against atmospheric re-entry, projector reflections on clouds or an aircraft confusion. That is a stronger conclusion than “we do not know”; it means the obvious families of explanation were considered and found insufficient for the described event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The 2018 contact with the witness added colour but did not greatly strengthen the case. GEIPAN recorded that the witness still remembered the observation well and emphasised features such as the object coming towards him, seeming to grow larger, the silence, the brief stop, the disappearance and the lack of anything visible once he went outside. He also remembered the experience as beautiful rather than frightening. GEIPAN treated these late additions cautiously, noting that they did not replace a proper field interview and cognitive interview at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Ecquevilly illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Yvelines_722ea1_ecquevilly_1993_case_78cc8a-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the doubts still matter

The most important doubt is simple: this is a one-witness case. No neighbour, family member or passer-by confirmed the sighting, and GEIPAN’s public case page says no trace was found and no other testimony was collected during the gendarmerie enquiry. That does not make the witness unreliable, but it means the case cannot be independently cross-checked in the way a stronger multi-witness or instrument-supported case could be.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-11-01335" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanECQUEVILLY (78) 15.11.1993 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The timing also matters. The witness had just woken up at about 4.30 am. GEIPAN considered the possibility of a hypnopompic hallucination, the kind that can occur in the first moments of waking and feel clear and real. The report did not say this was the explanation; in fact, it found no positive element in the file that predisposed the case towards hallucination. But without a GEIPAN field interview and cognitive interview at the time, it could not be excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete171.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanMicrosoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>
