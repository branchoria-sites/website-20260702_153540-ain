## Why Corrèze matters in French UFO history

Corrèze sits inside one of the most important national contexts for French UFO reporting: the 1954 wave, when local newspapers across France carried many reports of “flying saucers”, “cigars”, lights, close encounters and alleged occupants. In Corrèze, the name that stands out is Antoine Mazaud of Mouriéras, near Bugeat, whose story was reported as a meeting with the passenger of a cigar-shaped craft. The account entered the specialist UFO literature because it combined a named rural witness, a close-range “occupant” claim, gendarmerie involvement and a simple but memorable narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/10sep1954mourierasf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Correze" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Modern Corrèze cases look very different. GEIPAN, the official French group within CNES that collects, analyses, archives and publishes reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena, classifies sightings by how well they are explained and how much reliable information remains after investigation. Its national statistics show that only a small minority of published cases are classed as unidentified after investigation, while far larger shares are identified, probably identified, or left without enough data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span></span></span>

That difference is crucial for reading Corrèze fairly. The older material often comes from press reports, private catalogues and retrospective UFO compilations. The newer material often includes witness questionnaires, gendarmerie records, technical checking and explicit classification. The department’s UFO history therefore divides into two broad layers: a folklore-rich 1950s layer, and a documented official-record layer from the late 1970s onward.

## The 1954 Mouriéras case: Corrèze’s best-known UFO story

The Mouriéras case is the one Corrèze sighting most likely to appear in French UFO histories. According to several newspaper accounts reproduced in later case files, gendarmes from Bugeat heard rumours that Antoine Mazaud, a farmer from the hamlet of Mouriéras, claimed to have met the passenger of a “flying saucer”. Mazaud reportedly said that on 10 September 1954, at about 8.30 pm, while returning from his fields, he met an unknown helmeted man on a path about 1.5 km from his home. The man allegedly shook his hand, embraced him, made unintelligible sounds, then entered a cigar-shaped, unlit object around three to four metres long, which took off vertically and moved west with very little noise. The gendarmerie visited the site but, in the newspaper version, found no trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/10sep1954mourierasf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

The case matters because it contains several elements that made 1954 UFO stories powerful: a rural setting, an apparently sincere named witness, a very close encounter, an occupant figure, a small craft and some official checking. Later summaries also note that the story was repeated in multiple newspapers and UFO catalogues, which gave it a durability that many one-line reports never achieved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/10sep1954mourierasf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

The doubts are equally important. The surviving public record is largely mediated through newspapers and later UFO writers. The most dramatic details vary in tone and elaboration, and the absence of physical traces weakens the evidential value. A later sceptical or cautious reconstruction discusses a possible helicopter hypothesis but also notes difficulties with it, including the reported low noise, the small size of the object and the alleged passage under a power line. That leaves the case in an awkward position: not convincingly explained, but also not supported by the kind of independent physical or instrumental evidence that would allow a strong extraordinary claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/10sep1954mourierasf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Correze illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The 1954 local cluster: lights, cigars and weakly sourced reports

Mouriéras was not the only Corrèze-linked report from the 1954 wave. Local and specialist compilations list several other reports across the department, including Saint-Mexant, Ussel, Tulle, Espartignac, Albussac and Uzerche. These entries are useful as a map of local UFO interest, but their evidential quality varies sharply. Some are brief catalogue notices; others depend on press summaries; a few have later critical notes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://limousin-ovni.e-monsite.com/pages/historique.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: limousin-ovni.e-monsite.com">[e-monsite.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">limousin-ovni.e-monsite.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Historique OVNI Limousin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Historique OVNI Limousin</span></span></span>

The Uzerche report is a good example of how a vivid local sighting can become less mysterious under later scrutiny. A case file summarising press and UFO literature says witnesses saw a bright ball for about a quarter of an hour, changing from white to orange and red before disappearing. Later analysis considered the Moon a possible explanation, noting its position and phase on the relevant evening, while also acknowledging that the witnesses’ exact positions were not known well enough to confirm the explanation completely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/8oct1954uzerchef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

Another example is the Ussel “windscreen” case of June 1954. It was not a classic object-in-the-sky report: a driver travelling between Neuvic and Ussel reportedly heard a crack, saw his windscreen become striated, and later had the glass crumble. The case belongs to a wider 1954 “windscreen pitting” or “windscreen cancer” scare, which was variously attributed at the time to mystery causes, manufacturing defects, vandals or mass suggestion. Its value for Corrèze UFO history is not that it shows an unknown craft, but that it shows how the 1954 saucer climate absorbed ambiguous incidents that would normally belong to motoring, materials failure or local rumour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/12jun1954usself.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

The 1954 Brive-la-Gaillarde entry is even weaker. A later case note says an online UFO database listed a 23 September 1954 sighting at 9.45 pm, apparently derived from a Jacques Vallée computerised catalogue, but the same note states that the information is totally insufficient and carries no real strangeness. This is the kind of report that should be treated as a catalogue trace, not a robust case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/23sep1954brivelagaillardef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

## What GEIPAN’s Corrèze files show

GEIPAN’s Corrèze listings are the most useful public record for recent and semi-recent cases because they state the classification, the proposed explanation and the limits of the evidence. The published Corrèze entries visible in the case search include, among others, Ussac in 2010, Meyrignac-l’Église in 2011, Favars in 2019, Malemort-sur-Corrèze in 2020, Seilhac in 2021, the A20 between Brive and Limoges in 2022, Laroche-près-Feyt in 1977, Brive in 1979, Brive-la-Gaillarde in 1983 and Saint-Privat in 1993.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=29%2C5&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The pattern is striking. The best-documented modern cases are not “solved by dismissal”; they are usually explained by matching a witness description with a known sky phenomenon, flight path or lighting effect.

In Ussac on 25 June 2010, a witness saw a silent, very bright white spherical light for one to two minutes from a terrace. GEIPAN classified the case B, meaning probably identified, and concluded the witness had probably seen the International Space Station.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-06-02588" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

In Meyrignac-l’Église on 23 March 2011, a witness reported a very rapid white luminous ball that seemed to approach and then disappear upward, with no unusual sound and no other witnesses. GEIPAN classified the case B and considered it a probable Iridium satellite flare.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-03-02726" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

In Seilhac on 4 September 2021, a witness saw a bright horizontal “cigar” near the Big Dipper, apparently static for about a minute before moving slowly and disappearing behind a tree. GEIPAN classified it A, an identified phenomenon, after matching it to Transavia flight TO4159 from Oran to Paris; the perceived stillness was attributed to perspective at long range and low angular height.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-09-51247" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These cases are important because they show why UFO testimony can be sincere and still mistaken. A satellite, aircraft or flare can appear silent, strange, stationary, too bright, too low or too controlled when the witness lacks range, altitude and trajectory cues.

## Brive, aircraft and the aviation angle

Brive is the department’s clearest aviation anchor. In the 1979 Brive case, GEIPAN records several witnesses and a gendarmerie file. The main witness saw an oval object with fixed red and green lights and a strong yellow central light moving slowly and silently from east to west near Brive. The investigation identified it as a twin-engined F-Boom airliner that had landed at the nearby Brive aerodrome at 6.42 am and taken off again at 6.59 am; GEIPAN classified the case A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-01-00590" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That case is a useful warning against reading shape too literally. Aircraft lights can create an apparent oval or structured “object”, especially near dawn or dusk, and sound may be absent or delayed depending on wind, distance and local conditions. The case also shows why airport proximity matters. Brive-Souillac was officially opened to public air traffic in June 2010, while older Brive aviation infrastructure had already made the area an obvious place for aircraft-related misidentifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000022336618" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legifrance.gouv.fr">[Légifrance]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legifrance.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

The Seilhac 2021 case reinforces the same lesson at a greater distance. The witness initially thought of an aircraft but was troubled by the apparent lack of movement; GEIPAN’s explanation rested on the position of a known airliner and the perspective effect that can make a distant aircraft seem fixed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-09-51247" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Lights in the sky: satellites, meteors and projected beams

Several Corrèze cases fall into a broad “lights in the sky” category, but the explanations are different enough to matter.

The Malemort-sur-Corrèze case from 26 August 2020 involved multiple witnesses seeing nine bright objects moving quickly in pairs for about six minutes. GEIPAN classified it B and considered a Starlink satellite train the probable explanation, noting that the sighting occurred less than three weeks after the launch of Starlink train number 9 and that the objects were not yet well dispersed. The uncertainty was not about whether Starlink could fit the report, but about the exact angular height estimated by the witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58925?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;page=%2C8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The A20 case on Christmas Day 2022 involved a driver travelling from Brive towards Limoges near Uzerche who saw two round blue-white-grey forms in a cloudy sky for about 20 seconds. One appeared still while the other moved in multiple directions. GEIPAN classed the case B and favoured a projected light source, possibly a skytracer, because low cloud can act like a screen for moving light beams. The explanation remained probable rather than certain because there was only one witness, no photo or video, and no exact source of the projected light was confirmed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-12-51412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Brive-la-Gaillarde 1983 case was different again. An automobilist saw a red round object with a white trail falling vertically for about a second. GEIPAN classified it B and considered atmospheric re-entry probable, while also noting that no other information or trace was collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1983-11-01011" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These examples show why “it was a light” is not an explanation by itself. The key details are duration, angular motion, colour, grouping, direction, weather, altitude cues and whether independent witnesses, radar, photographs or known flight and satellite data exist.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Correze illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Correze_3e9490-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The unresolved cases are mostly unresolved because evidence is thin

Corrèze does have GEIPAN cases that remain unexplained in the practical sense, but most are class C, meaning the phenomenon is not identified because the available data are insufficient. That is not the same as a strong unexplained case after investigation. GEIPAN’s own classification system distinguishes lack of data from residual strangeness after a fuller investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Statistics &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Statistics &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Laroche-près-Feyt on 11 May 1977 is one such example. A witness saw two enormous orange luminous balls east of the house, apparently around 50 metres above the ground and strongly illuminating the area. After about a minute, the witness heard a sound like helicopter blades and saw an intense yellow light lighting the sector.     var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
