## Introduction

The Villers-lès-Luxeuil triangle case is the most interesting modern UFO file in Haute-Saône because it sits in an awkward middle ground: the obvious aviation explanations are real, nearby and worth taking seriously, but none of them fully matches what the witnesses reported. On 26 November 2019, two motorists returning from Vesoul towards Villers-lès-Luxeuil described a dark triangular or delta-shaped form above the village, with a strong white light and a fixed red light, apparently moving slowly and silently at low altitude. GEIPAN, the French space agency’s public unit for unidentified aerospace phenomena, eventually classified the case as D1: unidentified after investigation, with medium-to-strong consistency rather than proof of anything extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50872" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Villers Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
The case matters for Haute-Saône because Villers-lès-Luxeuil lies close to Luxeuil-Saint-Sauveur [air base]({{'air-base/' | relative_url }}), a place where aircraft explanations are not an afterthought but the first serious line of enquiry. GEIPAN examined a civil Robin DR400, Mirage 2000-5F activity from BA 116, and military drone possibilities. The result was not a dramatic confirmation of an unknown craft, but a careful unresolved file in which timing, perceived size, sound, shape and local aviation knowledge all pull against a simple answer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

## What the motorists said they saw

The account began before the better-known triangle sighting. According to GEIPAN’s published case summary, the husband, identified as T1, was driving behind his wife, T2, after the couple had collected a vehicle in Vesoul. Near Saulx-de-Vesoul, shortly after 17:00, he noticed a white light that seemed to be below a low cloud layer. He first considered an ordinary explanation, then became puzzled because the light appeared to rise vertically and disappear into the clouds. Less than ten seconds later, he saw it again above the area, tried to alert his wife with headlight flashes and a phone call, but she did not see that first phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50872" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The stronger part of the case came later, on arrival at Villers-lès-Luxeuil. Both witnesses then reported a large dark triangular structure, almost stationary above the village. GEIPAN’s summary says it had a bright white light at the front and a fixed red light. T1 stopped his car, turned off the radio and opened the windows to listen. He reported no engine sound. The object was described as gliding slowly, at an estimated altitude below 100 metres, for roughly 20 seconds before, according to T2, it tilted or rolled on itself and disappeared towards the north-north-west.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50872" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The official witness page preserves the features that make the sighting memorable: a black triangular or delta-wing-like 3D form, non-point lights, apparent stationarity, total silence, very cloudy conditions, no environmental effects and a local time of about 17:20. It also gives GEIPAN’s case scores: strangeness 0.55 and consistency 0.64. Those numbers are not a scientific measurement of exoticness; they are GEIPAN’s internal way of describing how unusual the report remains and how usable the witness data is after review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9754" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019 T2 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The witness detail is vivid, but not flawless. T2 described seeing the sides or thickness of the object, with two sides appearing metallic, while also saying the underside seemed “transparent” without seeing the clouds through it. GEIPAN noted a contradiction between one later description and a sketch supplied to investigators. The report also found that both witnesses, especially T2, had a tendency to overestimate angular size during comparison tests, a common problem in brief sky observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Villers Case illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the Robin DR400 explanation nearly works

The Robin DR400 is the most serious conventional explanation in the file. GEIPAN found that a Robin DR400/1B was present in the relevant time window, flying low, descending, and on approach to BA 116. Its route passed over the eastern part of the village at about 17:17, close to the reconstructed arrival time of the witnesses. It was about 330 metres above the ground, moving at roughly 150 km/h, and passed at around 700 metres from them. That is close enough to matter, but not so close that sound would necessarily dominate, especially for a small aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

The lights also fit better than they first appear. GEIPAN considered that the strong white light could have been the aircraft’s landing light, while the small red light could have been its left wing position light. From the witnesses’ viewing angle, the green right-side light would not logically be visible, while other lights could have been hidden by the fuselage or geometry of the approach. In short, the light pattern is not a weak match; it is one of the reasons the Robin stayed near the top of the explanation list.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

The timing work also strengthened the Robin hypothesis. The witnesses did not record an exact time, so GEIPAN reconstructed it using the route, phone records and street-light timing. Two calls between the witnesses occurred at 17:06 and 17:07. Depending on driving speed, the arrival at Villers-lès-Luxeuil was estimated between 17:17 and 17:20. That window fits the Robin’s passage at about 17:17 far better than the later Mirage track.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

Yet the Robin explanation also has the central weakness of the case: witnesses did not report a small white-and-orange light aircraft. They reported a large, dark triangular structure. GEIPAN stated that the apparent size of the Robin would have been about that of the full Moon, which is hard to square with T1’s statement that his outstretched hand would not have hidden the dark triangle. The report also says the Robin’s white-and-orange colouring should have appeared lighter than the cloud background, whereas the witnesses described something darker than the clouds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

So the Robin is not a bad debunking candidate. It is a partial explanation with real strengths: time, route, low altitude, landing light, red light and plausible quietness. What prevents it from closing the case is the reported silhouette. To accept the Robin as the answer, one has to accept a substantial perception error: a small aircraft glimpsed in dim, clouded twilight becoming, in memory and perception, a broad black triangle.

## Why the Mirage answer is tempting but weaker on timing

The Mirage 2000-5F hypothesis is attractive for one obvious reason: a Mirage is a delta-wing fighter. If a witness reports a triangular form near Luxeuil-Saint-Sauveur air base, a Mirage is not a fringe suggestion; it is exactly the kind of aircraft that should be checked. BA 116 has long been associated with Mirage 2000-5F operations, and the French defence ministry has described Mirage 2000-5F aircraft from the Luxeuil-based fighter group as part of French air defence deployments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gouv.fr/air/actualites/mirage-2000-5f-ba-luxeuil-saint-sauveur-engages-mission-eap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gouv.fr">[Ministère des Armées]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mirage 2000 5f ba luxeuil saint sauveur engages mission eap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministère des ArméesDes Mirage 2000-5F de la BA de Luxeuil-Saint Sauveur...6 Dec 2023 — Le 28 novembre, quatre Mirage 2000-5F du groupe...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN confirmed that four Mirage 2000-5F aircraft from BA 116 were involved in an exercise on 26 November 2019, taking off at 17:20 and landing around 18:20. One Mirage was radar-confirmed in the area between 17:30 and 17:40, travelling in a direction that could roughly match T1’s right-to-left perception. A banking Mirage could also present a more triangular outline than many ordinary aircraft. Dassault’s own data sheet gives the Mirage 2000 an approach speed of 140 knots, underlining that even in approach configuration it remains a fast jet rather than a hovering or crawling object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

The problems are substantial. GEIPAN’s reconstructed witness timing places the Villers sighting closer to 17:17–17:20, while the relevant Mirage track was closer to 17:30–17:40. The report says this makes the Mirage much less compatible than the Robin on time. It also found that the Mirage’s elevation, about 14 degrees, was much lower than the more-than-30-degree elevation described by the witnesses, and that its apparent size would be far smaller than the large triangle they reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

Sound is another difficulty. GEIPAN noted that a low-altitude Mirage is very noisy, and T1 had stopped his car, opened the window and turned off the radio precisely to listen. The witnesses also lived in the village and were familiar with Mirage operations from the nearby base. That does not make misidentification impossible, but it raises the bar: this was not a report by visitors with no local aviation context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

The Mirage therefore explains the word “triangle” better than the Robin, but explains the timing, silence and apparent immobility worse. It is a useful caution against treating shape alone as decisive. A delta-wing fighter can look triangular, but an actual explanation has to fit the whole event window, not just the silhouette.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Villers Case illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the drone idea was dismissed

The drone possibility sounds modern and plausible until the details are checked. GEIPAN found that a temporary restricted zone notice, SUP AIP 299/19, covered possible military drone transits between 7 November 2019 and 6 May 2020, a period that included the Villers sighting. The report mentions types such as MQ-9 Reaper or RQ-4B Global Hawk, and also notes that the nEUROn drone has a triangular form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

But the altitude band was the problem. GEIPAN stated that the authorised levels, from about 14,935 to 16,764 metres, were incompatible with a naked-eye sighting from the ground in the reported conditions, especially with total cloud cover below 3,000 metres. A drone at those levels would not be the low, large, silent form described above the village. GEIPAN also considered an amateur drone unlikely: a triangular consumer model was not identified, drone use near an air base would be restricted, and to match the apparent size it would have had to fly almost at rooftop level.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

This is an important part of the case because it shows that “drone” is not a magic modern explanation. A drone explanation still needs a model, a route, a legal or operational reason for being there, an altitude, and a size-and-light match. In this file, the military-drone clue was real enough to investigate, but it pointed to high-altitude transit rather than to a low triangle over Villers-lès-Luxeuil.

## Why GEIPAN left it unresolved

GEIPAN did not leave the Villers case unresolved because no conventional activity was found. It found too much aviation activity, not too little: a small civil aircraft in a good time window, Mirage activity nearby, and a military-drone airspace notice. The problem was that each explanation solved only part of the report.

The Robin DR400 fitted the time, trajectory and lights, but not the dark triangular form or the reported size. The Mirage 2000-5F fitted the triangular idea and local military setting, but was around ten minutes late, too low in elevation, apparently too small, too fast, and should probably have been audible. The military-drone possibility fitted the period on paper but failed on altitude and visibility. GEIPAN’s conclusion was therefore conservative: the case remained unidentified after investigation, classed D1 because of persistent strangeness and good witness consistency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

That classification should be read carefully. CNES describes GEIPAN as a unit that collects, analyses, investigates, publishes and archives UAP reports; it is not a body that proves extraterrestrial craft or carries out a national-security alert role. CNES’s own project page says GEIPAN was created under CNES, works with partners including the gendarmerie, police, Air and Space Force, CNRS and Météo-France, and that only a small percentage of cases remain unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

For Villers-lès-Luxeuil, “unidentified” therefore means exactly that: not confidently explained by the available evidence. It does not mean “confirmed non-human craft”, “secret aircraft”, or “definite misidentification”. The strongest fair reading is narrower and more interesting: this is a case where investigators had plausible aircraft candidates but could not make any of them absorb the whole witness account without leaving important mismatches.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Villers Case illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Saone_0bdc4b_villers_triangle_cas_ec9828-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What the case adds to Haute-Saône’s UFO history

Within Haute-Saône, the Villers file stands out because it is not just another vague light in the sky. It has a named place, a short event window, two direct witnesses, field reconstruction, phone-record timing, local street-light timing, weather context, nearby air-base checks and candidate aircraft tracks. It is exactly the kind of case where a simple explanation should have been possible if the witness account and aviation data lined up cleanly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

It also illustrates a recurring problem in department-level UFO history: the strongest cases are often not those with the most spectacular claim, but those with enough mundane detail to test. The Villers case became stronger after investigation in one sense, because GEIPAN documented why the leading explanations were incomplete. But it also became more modest. The report exposed memory issues, non-independent witnesses, angular-size overestimation and the absence of any photograph, video, radar return of an unknown object or additional independent witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete725.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete725</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVILLERS-LES-LUXEUIL (70) 26.11.2019...</span></span></span>

That balance is what makes the case useful. For believers, it is a modern official French file where ordinary aircraft explanations did not fully settle the matter. For sceptics, it is also a textbook example of how a striking triangle report can emerge in poor light near real aircraft activity, with witness perception and timing uncertainty doing much of the work. For Haute-Saône’s UFO record, it remains the department’s most persuasive modern unresolved file precisely because it is not clean, theatrical or easy. Its value lies in the unresolved fit between a believable local observation and aviation explanations that come close, but not close enough.
