## Introduction

The public Mayenne record is especially useful because it contains several “almost UFO” stories: a bright star that seemed extraordinary, fireworks mistaken for hovering lights, the International Space Station photographed as a moving luminous object, and one more unusual Aron report that GEIPAN could not use because the supporting details did not hold together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01060" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN+3GEIPAN+3GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Why Mayenne&#x27;s UFO Files Matter" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## What the Mayenne record actually shows

The clearest public tally comes from GEIPAN-derived case listings: Mayenne is shown with 10 recorded cases, divided into two class A cases, three class B cases, five class C cases and no class D cases. In GEIPAN terms, class A means explained without ambiguity, class B means the preferred explanation is very probable, class C means the file cannot be analysed because key information is missing, and class D means unexplained despite the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/mayenne" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on carteovni.fr.</span></span></span>

That matters because “not identified” does not always mean “mysterious after investigation”. In Mayenne, the weak category is mostly class C: reports where a date is uncertain, a witness is alone, no independent checks are possible, or the account contains contradictions. Those cases should not be treated as strong unknowns. They are better read as unresolved paperwork, not as evidence of an extraordinary phenomenon.

The department’s cases are scattered rather than clustered into a clear flap. The public list includes Château-Gontier, Bais, Pré-en-Pail, Lassay-les-Châteaux, Laval, Villiers-Charlemagne, Évron and Aron. Aron is the most recent small cluster, with two 2019 entries: one left in class C for lack of reliable information, and another explained as the International Space Station.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/mayenne" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr+2GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on carteovni.fr.</span></span></span>

## The most useful Mayenne cases to understand

### Bais, 1985: when a star becomes a UFO report

The Bais case is one of the simplest but most revealing Mayenne entries. On 21 February 1985, at about 10 pm, two witnesses reported a very bright yellow luminous ball that changed to vivid red, remained stationary and silent for roughly 20 minutes, then faded. The main witness and others reportedly saw it on following nights as well. GEIPAN classified the case as B and described it as probably the observation of a particularly visible star in a clear winter sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01060" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is a common pattern in serious UFO archives: the witness experience can be sincere and striking while the likely cause remains ordinary. A bright astronomical object low in the sky can appear to change colour because of atmospheric distortion, and a stationary light becomes suspicious when the observer expects aircraft-like movement. The Bais file therefore matters less as a mystery than as a textbook Mayenne example of how duration, silence and brightness can mislead.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Why Mayenne&#x27;s UFO Files Matter illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
### Lassay-les-Châteaux, 1990: the fireworks explanation that did not fully satisfy the witness

The Lassay-les-Châteaux case is more socially interesting. On 19 August 1990, at about 10.45 pm, a witness in a farmyard saw a green oval form above a building; he and his wife then saw red-orange lights for several minutes above the farm buildings. The gendarmerie was called, but the phenomenon had disappeared by the time officers arrived. Later tests used small fireworks, and a fireworks display had in fact taken place that evening at nearby Charchigné, in the same direction and time window. GEIPAN therefore favoured fireworks and classified the case as B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-08-01211" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The interesting detail is that the witness did not recognise the test fireworks as matching what he had seen. GEIPAN noted, however, that the test was not under the same conditions: the witness knew what he was about to see. This is a good example of a probably explained case that still leaves a human residue of doubt. The official explanation is plausible and grounded in time, direction and local activity, but the witness’s memory of the event remained resistant.

### Aron, 2019: two sightings, two very different outcomes

Aron provides Mayenne’s clearest modern contrast. On 28 March 2019, a single witness reported seeing a vivid red rectangle and orange discs forming a pyramid-like structure that rose into cloud, leaving a strange trace. GEIPAN described the report as having high strangeness but low consistency. The problem was not simply that the claim sounded unusual; it was that the meteorological and astronomical details supplied by the witness did not match the verified conditions for the stated date. GEIPAN kept the case in class C because it could not be reliably analysed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-04-50742" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Two months later, on 31 May 2019, another Aron witness photographed a bright spherical light moving in the night sky. GEIPAN classified that case as A, identifying it as the International Space Station. The movement, brightness and angular height were consistent with an ISS pass; the apparent mismatch in direction was attributed to a likely reversal of cardinal directions by the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-05-50885" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Together, the two Aron cases are a useful caution. One strange-sounding report remains weak because the supporting data collapse; one more ordinary luminous point becomes strong because the timing, motion and satellite track line up.

## Older and thinner files: why “unresolved” is not the same as “strong”

Mayenne’s older class C files show the limits of retrospective investigation. The Château-Gontier/Mée 1976 file involved reports around late September and early October 1976 of a bright red or white luminous object seen in the early morning or evening. The gendarmerie record includes descriptions of a fixed or slow luminous form, short viewing durations, and later attempts to locate further witnesses. GEIPAN’s public listing places the file in class C for lack of reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/46964" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan+2Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Pré-en-Pail 1986 case is stranger in texture but still weak evidentially. GEIPAN summarises it as a hardened brown circle of earth in a henhouse, about 1.10 metres across, with poor water absorption and little vegetation, alongside testimony from people who reported seeing an intense white column of light that June. The case is class C, meaning the available information was not enough for a firm analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1986-11-01102" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Laval 2008 is more typical of low-information sky reports. Two people saw two fast-moving white points around 7.45 pm on an uncertain April date; one witness also thought there might have been red and green flashing between the lights. GEIPAN noted that the sighting resembled a possible aircraft observation but lacked precise information, so it remained class C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-04-02282" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Villiers-Charlemagne 2009 is similar. A witness later reported a round white luminous form making unusual movements, without blinking or sound. GEIPAN considered possibilities such as a helicopter or nearby quarry activity but said the old, imprecisely dated, single-witness report had too little strangeness and too little reliable information to justify a full investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02757" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Why Mayenne&#x27;s UFO Files Matter illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Mayenne_6ca058-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why Mayenne has no strong public “unexplained” case

The absence of a class D case in the Mayenne public record is not proof that every witness saw something mundane. It means that, in the public GEIPAN-derived record, no Mayenne file currently combines enough reliable information with enough unexplained strangeness to remain officially unresolved after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/mayenne" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on carteovni.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s own method helps explain why. Since 2008, it has assessed cases partly through “strangeness” and “consistency”: how far the report is from known phenomena, and how much reliable, objective information is available. A spectacular story with one witness, uncertain timing and contradictory conditions can score high for strangeness but still fail as evidence. Conversely, a bright moving light with a precise time, direction and photograph may be easy to explain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN+2GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

For readers, this is the main lesson of Mayenne’s UFO history: the best cases are not necessarily the weirdest-sounding ones. The best cases are those with precise timing, multiple independent witnesses, stable direction data, photos or video that preserve context, and enough external information to test against aircraft, satellites, stars, planets, fireworks, meteors and local activity.

## How to read a Mayenne UFO claim today

A Mayenne UFO report is worth taking seriously at the level of witness experience, but it should be graded carefully at the level of evidence. The department’s public record suggests a practical reading scale:

**Strongly explained:** Évron 2015 and Aron 31 May 2019 are good examples. They had enough data for GEIPAN to identify astronomical objects or the ISS.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-10-09352" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Probably explained:** Bais 1985 and Lassay-les-Châteaux 1990 fit here. GEIPAN’s preferred explanations, a star and fireworks, are plausible, but the wording remains probabilistic rather than absolute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01060" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Not usable as evidence:** Laval 2008, Villiers-Charlemagne 2009, Pré-en-Pail 1986 and Aron 28 March 2019 show why class C should not be inflated. These are not solved, but they are also not strong unknowns; the files lack the reliable information needed to test the claim properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-04-02282" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN+3GEIPAN+3GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The best Mayenne page in a department-level UFO history is therefore not a page of sensational claims.
