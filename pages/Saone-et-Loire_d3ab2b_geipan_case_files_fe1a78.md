## Introduction

Saône-et-Loire’s GEIPAN files do not prove that structured craft have visited the department. They prove something more modest, but more useful: when local UFO reports are put through an official evidence system, many become ordinary objects seen in misleading conditions, while others remain unresolved mainly because the record is too thin to test. The department’s public GEIPAN trail includes identified or probably identified cases such as Joncy in 1976, Saint-Usuge in 1980, Mâcon in 2008, Oye to Varenne-l’Arconce in 2022 and Morey in 2024, alongside several C cases where the limiting factor is missing or unreliable information rather than a strong anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/46879?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan+3Geipan+3Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for GEIPAN Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
That makes these files valuable for readers who want more than folklore. GEIPAN, a unit of the French space agency CNES, collects, analyses, archives and publishes reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena; CNES also stresses that GEIPAN is not an organisation set up to prove alien life or advanced technology. Its public database is therefore best read as an evidence filter: it shows what survives investigation, what collapses under checking, and what cannot be judged because the file is incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes.fr.</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN grades A, B, C and D cases

GEIPAN’s classification system is the key to understanding Saône-et-Loire’s official UFO record. The letters are not excitement ratings. They describe the state of explanation after investigation. A means the phenomenon has been clearly identified. B means the explanation is considered probable. C means the phenomenon is not identified because the available information is insufficient. D means it remains unidentified after investigation, with later refinements separating D1 and D2 according to evidential strength and strangeness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">baisse cas d</span></span></span>

Two ideas sit behind the system: residual strangeness and consistency. “Strangeness” is how far the reported event remains from known explanations after checks have been made. “Consistency” is the quantity and reliability of the information available: witness detail, timing, direction, photographs, video, material traces, corroboration and the quality of the investigation record. GEIPAN says C and D cases can be revisited if new information later becomes available, which is an important warning against treating a classification as a dramatic final verdict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

For Saône-et-Loire, this distinction changes the whole reading of the archive. A C case is not a “stronger mystery” than a B case. It is usually a weaker file. A B case may still contain uncertainty, but GEIPAN has enough evidence to favour a natural or human-made explanation. A C case may sound intriguing in a witness account, yet be scientifically less useful because the time, direction, duration, distance, documents or corroboration are too poor to support a firm reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">baisse cas d</span></span></span>

This is why the department’s official record is more about method than myth. The most meaningful question is not “How many UFOs were reported?” but “What did the file allow investigators to test?” Once that question is asked, Saône-et-Loire looks less like a hidden hotspot and more like a local sample of a national pattern: ordinary lights, aircraft, the Moon, satellites, lanterns and poorly documented observations, with classification doing the hard work of separating explanation from uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=134%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="GEIPAN Files illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What the Saône-et-Loire list actually shows

The public GEIPAN database shows a spread of Saône-et-Loire cases from the 1970s to the 2020s. The named files include Joncy in 1976, Charolles in 1978, Louhans in 1979 and 1997, Saint-Usuge and Saint-Germain-lès-Buxy in 1980, Montpont-en-Bresse in 1984, Dompierre-les-Ormes in 1995, Mâcon in 2001, 2008 and 2009, Saint-Léger-sur-Dheune in 2002, Autun in 2014, Brion in 2016, Génelard in 2017, Oye to Varenne-l’Arconce in 2022 and Morey in 2024. The striking point is the mix of A, B and C outcomes, not a run of robust D cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=134%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan+2Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## From Joncy to Morey: resolved cases are not empty cases

Joncy, observed on 25 June 1976, is useful because it shows how an old rural case can sound more dramatic than the file ultimately supports. GEIPAN summarises the report as successive night-time observations of luminous phenomena accompanied by engine noise. The gendarmerie investigation concluded that the most plausible explanation was a vehicle passing near the witness’s farm, and the case is classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/46879?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The important point is not that the witness was foolish or dishonest. It is that the environment matters. A farm at night, moving lights, partial visibility and engine noise can combine into a convincing personal experience. In official terms, however, the case has little residual strangeness once a nearby vehicle accounts for the light and sound. Joncy therefore proves less about exotic craft than about how easily a local setting can turn a familiar source into a puzzling observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/46879?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Mâcon on 26 December 2008 shows a different sort of resolution. GEIPAN classifies the case B and lists the phenomenon type as an airliner. A B classification does not claim mathematical certainty; it means the retained explanation is probable after investigation. In a department crossed by road, rail and regional air routes, a distant aircraft seen at night can produce a report that feels stranger than it is, especially when witnesses lack reliable cues for altitude, distance and speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-12-01973" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The 2022 case from Oye towards Varenne-l’Arconce is especially helpful because it shows how a moving observer can misread the Moon. GEIPAN classifies it A and lists the phenomenon as the Moon. The case summary explains that the perceived horizontal and vertical movement was linked to the topography of a winding, rising road, while the temporary and final disappearances were consistent with cloud cover, low elevation and obstacles near the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51353" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Morey, observed on 4 August 2024 and updated in GEIPAN’s database in March 2026, brings the archive into the satellite era. GEIPAN classifies it A and lists the phenomenon as a satellite train, with low residual strangeness and relatively good consistency. The investigation report describes a bright, aligned, silent formation and matches it to the Starlink group 11-1 passage, including timing, direction, curved apparent path, disappearance into the Earth’s shadow and corroborating observations from elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61431" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Morey is a good modern counterweight to older weak cases. It had two witnesses and a partially usable video, so the file had enough structure to test. The conclusion was not “unexplained”; it was that a well-documented new kind of sky spectacle, a recently launched train of satellites, matched the report closely. That is exactly what a good official file can do: preserve the human report while showing why the most economical explanation is not the most exciting one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete830.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Microsoft Word</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="GEIPAN Files illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why weak documentation is not the same as mystery

Several Saône-et-Loire entries are C cases, including Dompierre-les-Ormes in 1995, Mâcon in 2001 and 2009, Saint-Léger-sur-Dheune in 2002, Chagny in 2013 and Génelard in 2017. GEIPAN’s public listings give the recurring reason: lack of reliable information. These cases are part of the department’s UFO record, but they are not the strongest evidence in it. ## What the files prove about witnesses

The Saône-et-Loire files do not show that witnesses are generally inventing stories. In fact, GEIPAN’s whole system is built around taking direct testimony seriously enough to standardise it. Its methodology says human testimony is central, but it also requires a technical questionnaire and may use sketches, photographs, videos or other material where available. This balance is important: the witness experience matters, but the witness experience is not automatically the same as the external cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Oye to Varenne-l’Arconce Moon case illustrates this well. The report was not dismissed as nonsense; it was reconstructed against road movement, terrain, cloud and lunar elevation. The explanation depends precisely on taking the witness’s setting seriously. The apparent movement of a low object can be created by the observer’s own motion along a winding road, especially when a familiar object is partly hidden and revealed by local topography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51353" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Morey adds another witness lesson. The observers reportedly described a striking luminous bar, and the file had enough detail for GEIPAN to compare the account with a specific satellite launch and pass. The witnesses’ description was not useless because it was explained; it was useful because its timing, appearance and direction allowed the explanation to be tested. In UFO work, a resolved case with good detail can be more valuable than an unresolved case with almost none.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete830.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Microsoft Word</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="GEIPAN Files illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Saone-et-Loire_d3ab2b_geipan_case_files_fe1a78-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What the files do not prove

The Saône-et-Loire GEIPAN record does not prove a local wave of alien craft, secret military platforms or a hidden aviation corridor of extraordinary events. The public database points instead to a patchwork of explainable cases, probable explanations and under-documented reports. Even nationally, GEIPAN presents itself as an organisation for collecting, analysing, archiving and informing the public about reports, not as a body claiming to identify extraterrestrial visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## What they really prove

What Saône-et-Loire’s GEIPAN files really prove is that classification is evidence, but not the evidence many people expect. The official trail shows that a local UFO record can be meaningful even when it does not contain a spectacular D case. It teaches readers how reports are sorted: clearly identified, probably identified, not analysable because of weak data, or still unexplained after stronger investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

They also prove that the same department can contain different kinds of UFO history. Joncy belongs to the older world of rural night sightings and gendarmerie notes. Mâcon 2008 belongs to probable aviation explanation. Oye to Varenne-l’Arconce shows the Moon becoming strange through road movement and landscape. Morey shows how satellite megaconstellations can create dramatic modern reports that are highly testable when timing and direction are known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/46879?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C150" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan+3Geipan+3Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Most of all, the files prove that “unidentified” is not a single condition. Sometimes it means a genuine residue after checking. Sometimes it means a probable aircraft, Moon, satellite or lantern. Sometimes it means the report arrived too late, too vaguely or too thinly to reconstruct.
