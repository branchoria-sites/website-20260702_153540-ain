## Introduction

That does not make the department uninteresting. Its cases show how ordinary-looking local reports can become “UFO” stories before investigation: aircraft lights seen from the ground, a bright balloon in daylight, a dark object glimpsed by a tired driver, a kite with a camera, and probable sky lanterns. ## What the official record actually contains

GEIPAN’s search index for Territoire de Belfort lists six relevant public cases in the department: Beaucourt on 2 April 1979, Belfort on 4 May 1981, Méroux on 26 September 2009, a Grandvillars-to-Delle road sighting on 14 April 2013, Valdoie on 24 December 2015, and Belfort on 21 January 2020. The official classifications are revealing: Beaucourt, Belfort 1981 and Belfort 2020 are classed A; Méroux and Valdoie are classed B; Grandvillars-to-Delle is classed C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=18&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

In GEIPAN language, class A means the phenomenon was identified after investigation, class B means it was probably identified, class C means the case cannot be identified because the information is insufficient, and class D is reserved for phenomena not identified after investigation. This distinction matters because a class C case is not the same as a strong mystery; it is often a thin file with too little data to test properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

For Territoire de Belfort, the pattern is therefore modest but useful. The public official file does not show a major “flap” comparable to better-known French waves, nor does it show a department full of high-strangeness, multi-witness, sensor-backed incidents. It shows a small set of reports, with the strongest official conclusions pointing towards conventional explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=18&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The Beaucourt case: a dramatic report that became an aircraft explanation

The most vivid older case is Beaucourt, reported on 2 April 1979. According to GEIPAN’s case summary, the gendarmerie received telephone calls at about 20:40 reporting an object over the commune emitting a bright light. Officers who went to the scene saw an orange luminous phenomenon moving across a clear sky towards the north-east, with flashing red lights and a fixed green light. On its face, this is exactly the kind of description that can sound impressive in a local UFO retelling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47976?page=140&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The investigation, however, found a conventional match: an aircraft, identified in the summary as a DC-9, was carrying out training flights in the region between about 20:30 and 21:15. GEIPAN classed the case A, meaning identified, and summarised it as bright lights at altitude caused by an aircraft on a training flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47976?page=140&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This case matters because it had several features readers often associate with a stronger report: multiple callers, gendarmerie involvement, an observed light pattern, and movement across the sky. Yet the light configuration itself — orange appearance, flashing red lights and a fixed green light — is also exactly the sort of detail that can strengthen an aviation explanation rather than weaken it. In Territoire de Belfort’s UFO history, Beaucourt is less a mystery than a cautionary example: official attention does not automatically mean an unexplained event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47976?page=140&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Territoire de Belfort illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Méroux in 2009: the bright silver “ball” and the balloon hypothesis

The Méroux case from 26 September 2009 is the department’s best-documented modern example because GEIPAN published a short investigation report. The witness reported seeing a very bright silver ball above Méroux, south-east of Belfort, at around 11:00 in the morning. It appeared still, then moved very slowly horizontally, and suddenly disappeared from view. The witness took photographs and later contacted GEIPAN.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete268.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete268</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete268</span></span></span>

The witness described good visibility, a clear sky, an east-north-east viewing direction, an estimated five-minute duration, a spherical shape, and a sudden disappearance compared to a bubble bursting. ## Grandvillars to Delle: why “unexplained” can mean “not enough to work with”

The Grandvillars-to-Delle case of 14 April 2013 is the one Territoire de Belfort file most likely to be misunderstood if readers look only at the classification. GEIPAN classed it C, not A or B. That means it was not identified, but because of insufficient information rather than because it survived a strong investigation as a robust unexplained event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The witness, a 30-year-old woman, was driving at about 01:30 when she saw, for a few seconds, a black sphere moving in a straight line before disappearing behind trees. The testimony page records a single object, spherical in form, black in colour, with an apparent size compared to a bowl. No other testimony was collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7946" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s own discussion is notably cautious. It stated that the black sphere did not fit the usual classic misidentifications, but also that the distance, straight-line movement and brief duration made the case relatively low in strangeness. The witness was alone, saw it only for a few seconds after a very long day, and GEIPAN explicitly left open possibilities such as an illusion, a night bird, or a fatigue-related visual problem. The gendarmerie report had not been received by GEIPAN at the time of the published summary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is a good departmental example of the difference between “unexplained” and “strongly unexplained”. A weakly evidenced case can remain unidentified because the data are too poor, not because the phenomenon was extraordinary. In a public-facing UFO history, the Grandvillars-to-Delle sighting should be described as unresolved but evidentially thin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Territoire de Belfort illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Belfort in 2020: a modern photograph, a witness, and a kite

The 21 January 2020 Belfort case is important because it shows how a contemporary report with a photograph can still resolve into a simple local explanation. At about 16:45 to 17:00, a witness saw a dark object from a window, with four coloured horizontal bands below it — red, green, blue and possibly yellow. The object continued moving in the sky with turning motions until nightfall, and the witness photographed it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN rated the case as having good consistency because there was a photograph and a high-quality witness account. But the witness later found the explanation: a kite in the Pépinière district of Belfort, fitted with a GoPro camera. GEIPAN classed the case A, with zero residual strangeness in the published file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This case is valuable because it avoids two common mistakes. Sceptics should not dismiss every report as careless: the witness took a photograph and tried to understand what had been seen. UFO enthusiasts, however, should not treat a photograph as automatically strong evidence. A photo can preserve an odd-looking shape while still lacking the scale, distance and context needed to identify it without local follow-up.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Valdoie, Belfort 1981 and the smaller official entries

Two Territoire de Belfort cases in the public index are thinner in accessible detail but still help define the department’s pattern. ## Why aviation context matters in this department

Territoire de Belfort has enough aviation background to make aircraft-related explanations especially relevant. ## What the department’s pattern does and does not show

The available evidence does not support a dramatic Territoire de Belfort UFO narrative. What the pattern does show is still meaningful. Several reports began with sincere observations of something odd: a bright object, a moving sphere, coloured bands, lights over a commune.
