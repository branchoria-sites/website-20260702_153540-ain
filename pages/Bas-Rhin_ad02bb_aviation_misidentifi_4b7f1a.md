## Introduction

[Bas-Rhin]({{'what-really-happened-in-bas-rhin-skies/' | relative_url }})’s UFO record is strongly shaped by ordinary aviation and optical misidentification. The department contains Strasbourg-Entzheim airport, busy approach paths across the Rhine plain, and the former Drachenbronn air-surveillance complex, while GEIPAN’s local files show several cases where apparently strange lights were later matched to aircraft, military refuelling, dashboard reflections, balloons, planets or rocket debris. The useful lesson is not that every Bas-Rhin report is trivial. It is that the best local investigations often turn on reconstruction: where the witness stood, what direction they faced, whether they were in a moving car, whether glass or a camera lens distorted the view, and whether flight or radar data match the timing. In the independent GEIPAN-derived Bas-Rhin listing, 45 cases are recorded: 12 class A, 21 class B, 11 class C and only one class D unexplained case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/bas-rhin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carte Ovni.fr OVN I dans le Bas-Rhin (67) — Carte Ovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carte Ovni.fr OVN I dans le Bas-Rhin (67) — Carte Ovni.fr</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Sky Explanations" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
## Why aircraft explanations matter so much in Bas-Rhin

Bas-Rhin is not an empty-sky department. Strasbourg-Entzheim sits at Entzheim, west-south-west of Strasbourg, with a single 05/23 runway and regular civil traffic; airport live information also shows how mixed the traffic can be, from domestic French routes to Mediterranean and European destinations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strasbourg_Airport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Strasbourg Airport</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Strasbourg Airport</span></span></span> That matters because a low, bright, slow-looking aircraft can become difficult to recognise when it is seen for only a few seconds, from a moving car, at dusk, or without a clear horizon reference.

The strongest local example is the 19 April 2019 case between Gresswiller and Mollkirch. A driver and passengers saw what seemed to be an enormous stationary craft near treetops, roughly seven kilometres away. GEIPAN classified it as A, identifying it as a Boeing 737 on approach to Strasbourg. The case is valuable because it shows two recurring traps at once: distance was hard to judge, and the witnesses’ own movement in the car helped create an apparent “pivot effect”, making the aircraft seem almost fixed against the landscape. GEIPAN also noted that the aircraft was in landing phase and moving more slowly than at cruise speed, which strengthened the illusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-04-50740" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A similar pattern appears in the 9 May 2018 Haguenau case. A witness at a rugby ground photographed a bright cylindrical “ball” and a dark object, with no sound heard. GEIPAN’s reconstruction found that the bright object matched an airliner moving from north-east to south-west, and that the aircraft’s fuselage reflected the Sun while the wings were not obvious. The dark objects were assessed as likely dirt or marks on the camera lens. This is a classic Bas-Rhin misidentification pattern: a photographed “cigar” or luminous cylinder may not be exotic at all, especially when only the fuselage catches sunlight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-05-50525" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Sky Explanations illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Strasbourg-Entzheim makes some sightings look stranger than they are

Airport context does not explain every report, but it changes how a reader should assess low lights, silent shapes and apparent hovering in the Strasbourg area. Aircraft approaching or leaving an airport can appear slow, almost motionless or oddly bright depending on angle. Landing lights can face a witness directly; fuselage reflection can hide wings; navigation lights can look like separate objects; and approach paths can produce the impression that a light is hanging over a fixed point.

The 19 November 2017 Fouchy-to-Urbeis files show how this can happen inside a single car journey. Two people saw coloured lights through the windscreen and described the phenomenon differently. GEIPAN split the event into two linked cases. For the front passenger, four points in a cross-like pattern, with one blinking in the middle, were matched to a Boeing 717 on approach to Strasbourg; GEIPAN noted that from a distance, through car glass, navigation light colours may not be properly distinguished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-11-50565" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Drachenbronn explains the surveillance setting, not every UFO claim

Drachenbronn has a special place in Bas-Rhin’s sky history because it was not a flying base in the ordinary sense. It was a radar and control site, built into the former Hochwald Maginot Line complex near Drachenbronn-Birlenbach, with a mission of air-surveillance over eastern or north-eastern France. Public descriptions of the former BA 901 stress that it housed the Centre de détection et de contrôle 05/901 and that a three-dimensional radar on the Hochwald heights dominated the Alsace plain and Rhine valley.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drachenbronn_Air_Base" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Drachenbronn Air Base</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Drachenbronn Air Base</span></span></span>

This background matters because it can tempt readers to overstate the radar angle. A department with a famous radar site is not automatically a department with radar-confirmed UFOs. In practice, the Bas-Rhin files that best illustrate radar’s value are not dramatic unknowns but solved cases: radar or flight data helped investigators connect odd visual reports to ordinary aircraft or military activity.

The 17 September 2025 Marlenheim case is the clearest example. A witness reported a large aircraft accompanied by smaller circular objects, then a second similar event. GEIPAN’s account says CAPCODA radar reconstruction showed an A330 MRTT tanker involved in in-flight refuelling with fighter aircraft. The fighter aircraft appeared as smaller “objects”, and the manoeuvre explained the witness’s impression of companions moving with, then turning away from, the larger aircraft. GEIPAN classified the case A, as military aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2025-09-51692" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is a useful corrective to a common UFO-reading mistake. Radar information does not only “confirm mysteries”; often it removes them. In Marlenheim, the oddity came from the witness’s correct perception that several things were flying together, but the interpretation changed once their scale, altitude and military refuelling pattern were reconstructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2025-09-51692" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Sky Explanations illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Bas-Rhin_ad02bb_aviation_misidentifi_4b7f1a-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Rocket debris shows how a national event can enter local memory

Bas-Rhin’s airport and radar setting is only part of the story. Some local “UFO” impressions came from far above normal aviation altitude. The major French example is the evening of 5 November 1990, when more than a thousand witnesses across France saw a large luminous phenomenon crossing a clear sky. GEIPAN’s later simulation describes it as the atmospheric re-entry of debris from the third stage of a Soviet Proton rocket, travelling at roughly 90 to 50 kilometres altitude and slowing from about 7 to 2 kilometres per second.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span></span></span>

The event is especially relevant to Bas-Rhin because contemporary reporting and later summaries describe a west-to-east track across France, with Strasbourg and Alsace among the regions where the spectacle was discussed. Le Monde’s archive account described the burning rocket debris entering the denser atmosphere on a path from Pau towards Strasbourg, while GEIPAN emphasised that the brightness made the phenomenon appear much lower and closer than it was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1990/11/14/les-debris-d-un-mystere-trois-jours-auront-suffi-pour-donner-une-explication-a-un-phenomene-etrange-apparu-dans-le-ciel-d-europe_3980116_1819218.html?srsltid=AfmBOopk-SqzEu3h1bxtfFyHB6MwQXacgNNSPaXLU3SQjZy2SLy5eVy4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lemonde.fr">[Le Monde.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lemonde.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Monde.fr Les débris d&#x27;un mystère Trois jours auront suffi pour</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Monde.fr Les débris d&#x27;un mystère Trois jours auront suffi pour</span></span></span>

## Recent solved cases show three repeating failure modes

Bas-Rhin’s solved files are not all the same. They show several distinct ways ordinary lights become UFO reports.

**The aircraft that looks stationary.** In the Gresswiller-Mollkirch case, a Boeing 737 on approach seemed huge and almost fixed above the landscape. GEIPAN explained this through the short duration of the observation, the aircraft’s approach speed, the witnesses’ movement in a car, and the “pivot effect” against the scenery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-04-50740" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**The aircraft that loses its wings.** In Haguenau, a photographed bright cylinder was identified as an airliner. GEIPAN’s explanation is practical: if sunlight reflects from the fuselage but not the wings, the aircraft may look like a wingless cigar or luminous capsule, especially when zoomed or seen at a distance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-05-50525" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**The cockpit, windscreen or camera that creates a false object.** The Fouchy-Urbeis driver’s sighting was traced to a dashboard reflection, while the Haguenau dark “objects” were assessed as lens dirt. These cases are important because photographs do not automatically strengthen a report. ## What radar and flight data can and cannot prove

Radar, flight trackers and official air-operation data are powerful in Bas-Rhin investigations, but they are not magic. They can match time, direction, altitude and movement. They can show that a reported light coincided with a known airliner, a military tanker, fighters, or an approach path into Strasbourg. They can also expose when a witness’s estimate of size or distance is physically unlikely.

Their limits are just as important. A radar match does not reproduce the witness’s emotional experience, and it may not explain every detail unless the optical conditions are reconstructed. Conversely, the absence of a public flight-tracker match is not proof of an unknown craft: military aircraft, transponder settings, low-level coverage, data gaps and delayed reporting can all complicate the picture. GEIPAN’s methodology explicitly weighs both “strangeness” and “consistency”, using witness detail, photographs, radar or other detection data where available, and classifying cases from A to D depending on how well the explanation survives investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## How this changes the reading of Bas-Rhin UFO history

The airport, radar and misidentification pattern makes Bas-Rhin’s UFO history more disciplined, not less interesting. The department’s record is not just a list of lights; it is a set of tests for how ordinary aerial activity becomes extraordinary testimony. Strasbourg-Entzheim supplies plausible aircraft candidates. Drachenbronn explains the region’s long air-surveillance culture. GEIPAN’s case files show how photographs, road geometry, windscreens, radar reconstructions and flight paths can turn a puzzling report into a specific explanation.

This also sharpens the contrast with Bas-Rhin’s genuinely harder files. If many reports can be resolved through aircraft, reflections, balloons, Venus or rocket debris, then the remaining unresolved case at Sélestat in 1994 should not be treated as just another vague light in the sky. It stands out precisely because so much of the surrounding department record is explainable or weakly documented. CarteOvni’s Bas-Rhin summary lists only one class D case out of 45 GEIPAN cases, while GEIPAN’s own classification system reserves D for phenomena not identified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/bas-rhin" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Carte Ovni.fr OVN I dans le Bas-Rhin (67) — Carte Ovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Carte Ovni.fr OVN I dans le Bas-Rhin (67) — Carte Ovni.fr</span></span></span>
