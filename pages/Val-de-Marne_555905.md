## What the official record says about Val-de-Marne

The most important source for Val-de-Marne is GEIPAN, part of the French space agency CNES. GEIPAN’s role is to collect, analyse, investigate, publish and archive reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena, often using witness questionnaires, photographs, videos, sketches, weather data and, where relevant, information from gendarmerie, aviation or other institutional partners.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/histoire-du-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Val-de-Marne_555905-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for What Really Happened in Val de Marne&#x27;s UFO..." loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
GEIPAN’s classification system matters because it prevents every strange report from being treated the same way. Class A means the phenomenon was identified after investigation; class B means it was probably identified; class C means the case remains unidentified because the data are too poor; and class D means it remains unidentified after investigation. GEIPAN also assesses “consistency”, meaning the quantity and reliability of available information, and “strangeness”, meaning how hard the residual phenomenon is to fit to a known explanation after checks are made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Across France, GEIPAN’s own dynamic statistics show that only a small minority of published, classified cases remain unidentified after investigation. Its statistics page lists 28% as class A, 38.8% as class B, 30.1% as class C, and 3.1% as class D. Those national figures are a useful warning for Val-de-Marne: an official UFO file is not automatically evidence of an extraordinary craft, and even an unexplained classification does not prove what the object was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Independent mapping based on GEIPAN data currently lists 40 Val-de-Marne cases, including 10 class A, 17 class B, 12 class C and 1 class D, plus meteor entries. That independent count should be read as a guide to the public dataset rather than a separate official statistic, but it fits the broader pattern: most Val-de-Marne reports are explained or probably explained, while only one stands out as a published unexplained case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/departement/val-de-marne" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on carteovni.fr.</span></span></span>

## The Choisy-le-Roi case: the one that remains genuinely unresolved

The central Val-de-Marne case is the Choisy-le-Roi sighting of 21 May 2012. According to GEIPAN, an automobilist noticed a luminous phenomenon at about 22:12 in a covered urban sky. After stopping at a red light, the witness described a cylindrical form, a strong light emerging from small window-like openings, an orange luminous sphere around the object, a stationary phase above the road, then an ascending movement during which the shape reportedly changed into an inverted V and turned white before disappearing into cloud. The total observation lasted about 15 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.201221 May 2012 — Observation d&#x27;un objet volant silencieux stationnaire puis en ascension; changement de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2012</span></span></span>

GEIPAN classified the case as D, more specifically D1 in its summary wording: an object of unknown nature. The reasons are clear from the file. The reported shape was unusual, the apparent stop above the road was difficult to reconcile with a simple passing object, and the later change of form and colour did not fit neatly with the main mundane hypothesis considered, a festive balloon. GEIPAN judged the testimony to have good consistency because it was detailed, but also noted the major weakness: it came from a single witness and had no photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.201221 May 2012 — Observation d&#x27;un objet volant silencieux stationnaire puis en ascension; changement de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2012</span></span></span>

That last point is crucial. Choisy-le-Roi is interesting because the report is precise, not because it is conclusive. In a dense urban area, one might expect a striking object above a road to be noticed by other drivers, pedestrians or residents. GEIPAN itself raises that difficulty. The case therefore sits in the narrow middle ground that makes UFO history difficult: it is not easily explained from the witness account, yet it lacks independent corroboration strong enough to push it beyond a carefully worded “unidentified after investigation”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.201221 May 2012 — Observation d&#x27;un objet volant silencieux stationnaire puis en ascension; changement de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2012</span></span></span>

For a public reader, the best interpretation is modest. The Choisy-le-Roi case is Val-de-Marne’s strongest official UFO entry because it survived GEIPAN’s review without a satisfactory explanation. ## The Maisons-Alfort lantern reports show how dramatic sightings can shrink under investigation

The Maisons-Alfort sighting of 27 June 2011 is a useful counterweight to Choisy-le-Roi because it was locally vivid but officially treated as probably explained. GEIPAN’s file says two people observed an orange luminous phenomenon in the evening, moving in a straight line above them, silent, for about one minute. ## Recent Val-de-Marne cases are mostly lessons in misidentification

Several recent Val-de-Marne files are especially valuable because they show exactly how everyday sky phenomena become UFO reports.

In Champigny-sur-Marne on 4 July 2023, a witness saw what looked like a slow “explosion” in the early morning sky for about ten minutes and took three photographs. GEIPAN classified the case as A after concluding that the object was the Moon under unusual conditions: full, near perigee, partly masked by clouds, yellow-amber in colour and apparently stationary to the naked eye. The file is a good example of how an astronomical object can look unfamiliar when weather, timing and expectation line up.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-07-51450" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

In Nogent-sur-Marne on 9 October 2023, a witness described and photographed a rainbow-coloured ball near the Sun. GEIPAN classified the case as A, identifying it as an iridescent cloud. The important point is that “strange-looking” is not the same as “unexplainable”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51477" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why aviation context matters in Val-de-Marne

Val-de-Marne is part of the dense Paris aviation environment. Paris-Orly Airport lies partly in Val-de-Marne and serves the Paris metropolitan area, with official Paris Aéroport pages giving live flight and airport information for Orly. The French Civil Aviation Authority’s regional structure also explicitly includes Val-de-Marne within its northern civil aviation area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.parisaeroport.fr/en/passengers/orly-airport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: parisaeroport.fr">[Paris Aéroport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">parisaeroport.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on parisaeroport.fr.</span></span></span>

This does not mean every Val-de-Marne UFO report is “just a plane”. That would be too crude. It does mean aircraft explanations deserve careful attention because witnesses in places such as Orly, Villeneuve-le-Roi, Choisy-le-Roi, Créteil, Maisons-Alfort, Cachan and Champigny-sur-Marne are observing skies affected by approach paths, departures, holding patterns, reflections, aircraft lights and changing viewing angles. A line of lights can look like a structure; a landing aircraft can appear stationary; cloud can hide or reveal lights in ways that seem like sudden acceleration or disappearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.parisaeroport.fr/en/passengers/orly-airport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: parisaeroport.fr">[Paris Aéroport]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">parisaeroport.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on parisaeroport.fr.</span></span></span>

Drone context also matters, especially for more recent reports. French public guidance requires many drone operators to register with the DGAC, and wider European and French guidance stresses the need to check local airspace restrictions. In a department close to Paris and Orly, legal and practical airspace constraints make casual drone explanations possible in some visual cases but not something to assume without evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F34630?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: service-public.gouv.fr">[Service Public]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">service-public.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

For investigators and readers, the aviation setting changes the standard of evidence. Without that, many reports will naturally fall into GEIPAN’s class C: interesting but not solvable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/what-did-i-see/step-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Val-de-Marne_555905-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="What Really Happened in Val de Marne&#x27;s UFO... illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Val-de-Marne_555905-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Val-de-Marne_555905-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What weakens and strengthens a Val-de-Marne UFO claim

The strongest Val-de-Marne material is not necessarily the most dramatic. It is the material with clear timing, stable witness description, independent corroboration, imagery, environmental checks and a transparent investigation. Choisy-le-Roi is important because the description is specific and GEIPAN found it hard to fit with a known object, but it is weakened by the absence of photographs and the lack of additional witnesses in a busy urban setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.201221 May 2012 — Observation d&#x27;un objet volant silencieux stationnaire puis en ascension; changement de...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2012</span></span></span>

## What Val-de-Marne adds to French UFO history

Val-de-Marne’s UFO record matters precisely because it is not spectacular. It shows how UFO history works in an ordinary French department: reports arrive from credible people in everyday settings; some sound dramatic at first; investigators compare them with known objects and conditions; most are explained or downgraded; a few remain unresolved because the observation is too strange or the evidence too limited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/histoire-du-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The department also shows why local UFO history should be read through place. Val-de-Marne is urban, close to Paris, crossed by busy skies, and partly tied to the Orly airport environment. That makes aircraft, lanterns, satellites, cloud effects, the Moon and reflections especially important recurring explanations. It also means that a genuinely unexplained report has to survive a crowded field of plausible ordinary causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.parisaeroport.fr/en/passengers/orly-airport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: parisaeroport.fr">[parisaeroport.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">parisaeroport.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on parisaeroport.fr.</span></span></span>

The best short answer is this: Val-de-Marne has one notable official unexplained UFO case, Choisy-le-Roi in 2012, but the wider departmental pattern is dominated by explained, probably explained and data-poor sightings. Its value is not as a dramatic UFO legend zone, but as a clear example of how French official investigation turns local surprise into classified evidence: sometimes solved, sometimes weakened, and occasionally left genuinely open.
