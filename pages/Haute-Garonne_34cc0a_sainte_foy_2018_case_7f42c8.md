## Introduction

The 8 July 2018 sighting at Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières, west of Toulouse, stands out because it is not an old newspaper tale or a dramatic local legend. It is a modern [GEIPAN]({{'geipan/' | relative_url }}) case with a long official file, a detailed witness statement, sketches and maps, an interview, expert review and several tested explanations. Yet it remains unresolved. GEIPAN classifies the case as category D, with a recorded strangeness score of 0.55 and a consistency score of 0.72, meaning the report was considered substantial enough to analyse but not strong enough to prove what was seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-07-50618" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 2018 Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">
Its importance in [Haute-Garonne]({{'what-makes-haute-garonnes-ufo-record/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history is precisely that balance. The case is neither a confirmed extraordinary object nor an easily dismissed anecdote. It is the department’s clearest recent example of a puzzling report that survived a serious official investigation while still depending on one human observation, with no photograph, video, physical trace or decisive radar confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

## What the witness reported on 8 July 2018

At about 11.30 pm on Sunday 8 July 2018, the witness was in a residential garden in Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières, a commune in Haute-Garonne to the west of Toulouse. GEIPAN records that the witness phoned within minutes of the observation, leaving a message at 11.41 pm. In that first account, he described a brief, silent passage in the area of the Great Bear, with four aligned lights that appeared to change into a triangular form before vanishing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

The later, fuller account became much more detailed. GEIPAN summarised the whole observation as lasting about twenty seconds and unfolding in five phases. First, about one minute after the witness saw what he took to be a shooting star, five or six bright points appeared and moved downwards slightly in a cascading effect. Then four points appeared in an aligned formation, seeming to reveal a dark rectangular shape with orange-dark tones. The shape then appeared to shift from vertical to horizontal movement and from rectangle to triangle, before an extremely rapid acceleration in the fourth phase. In the final phase, the form seemed to return to a rectangular appearance, slow down and fade into the night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-07-50618" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The witness reported no sound and no environmental effect. GEIPAN’s testimony page records a single main witness, aged 50, with another person present who did not see the phenomenon because events moved too quickly. The recorded conditions were a clear or night-clear sky, an urbanised/residential setting, multiple light points, white colour, and apparent rectangular and triangular forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9532" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is one reason the case is compelling but also fragile. It has a prompt initial report, then a rich later reconstruction, but the phenomenon itself was short-lived. GEIPAN noted that the key fourth phase was estimated at only one to two seconds, and that the witness was moving his gaze and dealing with garden obstructions during part of the observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="2018 Case illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## Why the documentation is stronger than most local cases

The Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières file is stronger than a typical brief light-in-the-sky report because it did not stop at a witness form. The witness first contacted GEIPAN shortly after the sighting, later supplied a typed free account, ten graphic reconstructions, maps showing the viewing position and approximate path, and an annotated sky map. GEIPAN then obtained a technical questionnaire and held a meeting with the witness on 11 December 2018, including environmental photographs, a technical reconstruction and a cognitive interview.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

## The explanations GEIPAN tested

GEIPAN did not classify the case as unexplained because no ordinary explanations were considered. The investigation tested seven main possibilities: a train of satellites, atmospheric re-entry or fragmentation, fireworks, a single aircraft, several aircraft in formation, a retinal persistence effect caused by strong lights before the observation, and drones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

Some explanations weakened quickly. A satellite train did not fit the date well: the first operational Starlink train had not yet launched, and GEIPAN’s space-surveillance checks did not find an object matching the reported group of lights at the relevant time. Atmospheric re-entry was also considered unlikely because no re-entry over France was identified for that date, and the reported lights were stable, white-golden and without a trail, unlike the variable colours and behaviour normally expected from re-entry debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

Fireworks were plausible enough to consider because the sighting occurred in early July near Toulouse, but GEIPAN could not identify a matching professional display in the relevant area and time window. The long apparent horizontal movement after the third phase also did not fit well. Drones were tested too, but GEIPAN judged them very improbable: if the movement and angular spread were interpreted as nearby drones, they would have had to be extremely close, close enough that the witness should probably have recognised them or heard their motors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

The most serious ordinary explanation was aircraft, especially several aircraft in formation. GEIPAN found that this was the most coherent hypothesis overall because it could account for changing apparent shapes, lights seen in alignment, and perspective effects. But two points counted heavily against it: the reported angular distance covered in a few seconds implied either very high speed or considerable proximity, yet no noise was heard; and the available radar traces did not show enough aircraft in the right spatial arrangement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

The Patrouille de France idea was checked separately because the date was close to the 14 July period and its aircraft have lights that might be relevant. GEIPAN records that the unit said night flying rules made that explanation impossible for the display team, and its schedule placed it away from the Toulouse region that evening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="2018 Case illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_2018_case_7f42c8-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The missing radar evidence is the case’s biggest weakness

The most important doubt in the file is not that GEIPAN ignored radar; it is that the best radar opportunity was not available when it mattered. GEIPAN requested a traffic radar return from the French air operations centre in October 2018, but the report states that GEIPAN did not have the resulting radar map. It may not have been received, may not have been followed up, or may have been misfiled. GEIPAN explicitly calls this regrettable, especially because the witness himself recognised that radar confirmation would be the one thing capable of strengthening his account decisively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

Later checks helped, but did not fully repair that gap. GEIPAN did obtain radar data around Toulouse from the Blagnac control centre, but the report says the time window may have been slightly too late compared with the observation. Space-surveillance checks through CNES and GRAVES found no matching low-orbit object or re-entry, which helps reject some space-related explanations, but that is not the same as proving what was in the lower atmosphere over the witness at 11.30 pm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>

This is why the case remains unresolved rather than extraordinary. The official investigation is strong enough to rule out several simple explanations, but not strong enough to replace them with a confirmed alternative. The missing or late radar material leaves the most realistic sceptical pathway — aircraft seen in an unusual geometry — neither fully proven nor fully dead.

## What D1 means and what it does not prove

GEIPAN’s D category means a phenomenon remains unidentified after investigation. Its D1 subcategory is important: GEIPAN describes D1 cases as strange but of medium consistency, typically involving a single testimony with no photograph or video. D2 is reserved for more strongly supported cases, such as those with several independent witnesses, images, video or physical traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That distinction fits Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières closely. The witness account was detailed, the consistency was judged good, and GEIPAN did not dismiss the witness’s coherence. But the case still rests on one person’s perception of a very brief, complex event in a night sky without firm distance cues. GEIPAN itself warned that estimates of distance, size, duration and speed in the sky are often uncertain because the observer lacks stable reference points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete679</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete679</span></span></span>
