## Why Creuse produces quiet but revealing UFO reports

Creuse is one of France’s least populated departments, with INSEE estimating its population at about 115,000 in the mid-2020s. That matters for UFO history because low population density affects both sides of the evidence: there may be less light pollution and more skywatching opportunity, but also fewer independent witnesses when something unusual appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.insee.fr/en/statistiques/serie/001760104" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: insee.fr">[Insee]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">insee.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Population estimates</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Population estimates</span></span></span>

The department’s rural character also changes how sightings feel. A distant aircraft light or balloon can look more isolated and more mysterious when there are few urban reference points, little traffic noise and a broad dark horizon. Nearby Limousin landscapes are promoted for starry skies, and the Millevaches area has been recognised for its unusually preserved night sky, which helps explain why celestial and aerial phenomena may stand out sharply to observers in and around this part of central France.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tourisme-creuse.com/en/vassiviere-lake/discover/100-natural/starry-sky/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tourisme-creuse.com">[Tourisme Creuse Limousin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tourisme-creuse.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tourisme Creuse Limousin Starry sky</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tourisme Creuse Limousin Starry sky</span></span></span>

This does not mean Creuse is a UFO “hotspot” in the sensational sense. The public GEIPAN record instead suggests a sparse, uneven pattern: isolated reports, often with limited witness numbers, and no strong public evidence of a major department-wide flap comparable with better-known French waves. That sparseness is itself important. In UFO work, a thin record should not be inflated into a legend; it should be treated as a warning that the available evidence may be too limited to support a strong conclusion.

## The official frame: how GEIPAN handles Creuse reports

GEIPAN is part of CNES, the French space agency, and was created to collect, analyse, archive and publish witness accounts of unidentified aerospace phenomena. CNES states that GEIPAN was created in 1977, works with partners including the gendarmerie, police, aviation and scientific bodies, and publishes documented accounts so the public can see both the report and the outcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span></span></span>

The terminology is worth pausing over. GEIPAN avoids the everyday word “UFO” because it can imply a physical craft or an alien association before the evidence has been assessed. Instead it uses PAN, or UAP in English, which simply means an unidentified aerospace phenomenon at the reporting stage. GEIPAN’s own mission statement stresses that it is not an organisation trying to prove extraterrestrial life or futuristic technology; its main task is to explain observations where possible and document the remainder.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

For a department such as Creuse, this official frame is especially useful because it prevents over-reading. GEIPAN’s classification system separates clear [explanations]({{'explanations/' | relative_url }}), probable explanations, non-analysable cases and unexplained cases. Its public figures show that only a small minority of investigated cases remain unidentified after investigation, while a large share are either clearly or probably identified, or cannot be analysed because the data are insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span></span></span>

The gendarmerie also matters because it is often the local doorway into the system. The French gendarmerie explains that once witness statements are taken, they can be passed to GEIPAN, but it also stresses a central difficulty of UFO evidence: witness accounts may be affected by perception errors, emotional response, memory limits and mistaken estimates of distance or trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie Nationale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span><img src="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="What Do Creuse UFO Reports Really Show? illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The 1988 Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois case: interesting, but too thin to carry much weight

The oldest prominent Creuse case in the public GEIPAN record is Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois, observed on 13 October 1988. According to GEIPAN’s summary, a witness saw a yellow luminous ball, roughly the apparent size of a car headlight, making rapid back-and-forth movements for about half an hour at around 8.30 pm. The witness’s spouse also reportedly saw the phenomenon, and no sound was detected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-10-01150?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At first glance, that sounds like the kind of report that attracts attention: a luminous object, unusual movement, two witnesses and a fairly long duration. But GEIPAN classifies the case as category C, meaning it is not analysable because reliable information is lacking. Its published page is explicit that no other information could be gathered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-10-01150?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That classification changes how the case should be read. A category C file is not the same as a strong unexplained case. It does not say “ordinary explanations failed”; it says the record is too weak to test them properly. Without precise direction, elevation, weather, astronomical checks, possible aircraft paths, local light sources or a fuller witness record, the dramatic parts of the story remain claims rather than evidence that can be weighed.

The Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois case therefore matters less as a landmark mystery than as a cautionary example. It preserves a local report from the late 1980s, but it also shows why older UFO accounts often become fragile: the memory may be sincere, the witnesses may be honest, and the phenomenon may still be impossible to assess because the supporting data were not collected or were not preserved in enough detail.

## The 2018 Sermur photograph: how an aircraft became a rectangular “structure”

The 2018 Sermur case is the clearest example in Creuse of a puzzling report becoming explainable through technical analysis. On 6 August 2018, at about 10 pm, a witness looking at the stars from a hill above Sermur noticed five close, aligned lights. After taking and enlarging a photograph, the witness saw what seemed to be a flat rectangular structure made up of white luminous rectangles bordered by red points. GEIPAN classified the case as A, meaning explained without ambiguity, and identified the likely cause as a passenger aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-08-50680" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The crucial evidence was not simply “it looked like a plane”. GEIPAN’s reasoning was more specific. The lights in the photograph were symmetrical; their colours matched aircraft lighting; the red points repeated at a rhythm consistent with anti-collision lights; and a green continuous trace matched the starboard navigation light of an aircraft. The reported direction also mattered: a plane moving north to south to the east of the witness would show the side on which a green starboard light would be visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-08-50680" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The 2024 N145 sighting: a Creuse UFO that became a balloon case

The most recent notable Creuse case in GEIPAN’s public record took place on 15 September 2024, when a motorist travelling on the N145 between Saint-Priest-la-Plaine and La Souterraine saw a small grey point in the sky at about 8.42 pm. The witness photographed it and later submitted a technical questionnaire and image to GEIPAN. The case was updated in July 2025 and classified as A: explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51600" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN identified the object as a crewed gas balloon from team LTU-1 in the 67th Gordon Bennett Cup. The investigation used the witness’s photograph to determine the observation direction and compared it with real-time tracking of the race. GEIPAN concluded that the balloon was present in the relevant direction at the relevant time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51600" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is a particularly instructive Creuse case because the object’s “strangeness” came from distance and motion. GEIPAN noted that a gas balloon can appear rounded and may not show its basket when seen from far away. It also explained the apparent immobility as an optical effect linked to the witness’s own movement and the distance to the balloon, even though the balloon was moving at about 46 km/h.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51600" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The wider aviation context strengthens the explanation. The Fédération Aéronautique Internationale described the 2024 Gordon Bennett event as the 67th edition of a long-distance gas balloon race launched from Münster, Germany, with teams heading south across Europe. The FAI also describes the race’s simple objective: to fly the furthest non-stop distance from the launch site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fai.org/news/follow-balloons-67th-coupe-aeronautique-gordon-bennett" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fai.org">[FAI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fai.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">follow balloons 67th coupe aeronautique gordon bennett</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">follow balloons 67th coupe aeronautique gordon bennett</span></span></span>

For Creuse UFO history, this case is a good modern contrast with Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois. Here there was a photograph, a date and time, a precise road corridor, known aerial traffic and an external tracking source. Those details allowed an explanation. The difference was not that the 2024 witness was more credible than the 1988 witness; it was that the 2024 report contained enough checkable information.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="What Do Creuse UFO Reports Really Show? illustration 2" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-2-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-2-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## What the Creuse cases show about evidence

Creuse is not a department where the public record supports sweeping claims about alien craft, military secrecy or repeated close encounters. Its best-documented cases instead show three evidence levels that readers should keep separate.

**Explained cases are still worth studying.** Sermur and the N145 sighting are not failures of UFO research; they are successful investigations. They show how a report can begin as a sincere mystery and end as a documented misidentification of an aircraft or balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-08-50680" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Weakly documented cases are not the same as unexplained cases.** Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois is intriguing because the reported movement and duration were unusual, but GEIPAN’s category C classification means the file lacks enough reliable information for analysis. That should make the reader cautious, not more excited.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-10-01150?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Are there signs of a Creuse flap?

On the public evidence available, Creuse does not show a clear, well-documented UFO flap in the way the term is usually used: a short period with many independent reports of similar phenomena across a defined area. The GEIPAN cases most visible for the department are separated by decades and have different evidential outcomes: a non-analysable luminous ball in 1988, an aircraft photograph in 2018, and a gas balloon in 2024.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-10-01150?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That does not mean residents have never reported other strange sights. It means the reliable public trail is thin. This distinction matters because UFO folklore often grows when scattered reports are treated as a pattern before the pattern has been demonstrated. In Creuse, the better reading is conservative: there are isolated cases, some investigated officially, but not enough public evidence to claim a sustained local wave.

## How later reporting has affected the Creuse record

Later reporting has generally weakened the more spectacular interpretation of Creuse sightings rather than strengthened it. Sermur became less mysterious once the photograph was analysed as a passenger aircraft. The N145 sighting became less mysterious once the Gordon Bennett balloon track was matched to the witness’s observation. Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois did not become stronger with time; it remained limited by missing information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-08-50680" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That is a meaningful outcome. In UFO history, the most honest archive is not one that preserves only mysteries. It is one that also preserves the explanations, the dead ends and the cases that cannot be responsibly judged. Creuse’s record is useful precisely because it contains all three: an explained aircraft, an explained balloon and an older report that cannot be properly analysed.

The department’s UFO history is therefore best understood as a small evidence laboratory. It shows how sincere witnesses can report real experiences without those experiences requiring exotic explanations. It also shows why official files, even when unglamorous, are valuable: they leave behind dates, classifications, documents and reasoning that can be checked rather than merely retold.<img src="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="What Do Creuse UFO Reports Really Show? illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{"/assets/images/Creuse_73fb1d-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">
## The balanced verdict on Creuse

Creuse should not be presented as a major French UFO centre. The public record does not support that. Its importance lies in something quieter and more useful: it shows how rural sightings are investigated, how weak cases should be labelled, and how ordinary aviation can look extraordinary under the right conditions.

The 1988 Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois report remains locally interesting but evidentially weak because GEIPAN could not gather enough reliable information. The 2018 Sermur case is a strong example of a photographic aircraft misidentification. The 2024 N145 case is a strong example of a tracked balloon explaining a puzzling visual report. Together, they make Creuse a department where the best lesson is not mystery, but method: start with the witness’s experience, preserve the details, test ordinary explanations, and avoid turning gaps in the evidence into certainty.
